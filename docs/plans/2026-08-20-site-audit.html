Picsel site audit

What the site ships, what search engines see, what a visitor actually reads, and what is left in the repo that nothing uses.

20 August 2026 Branch: work-ring 28 pages built Phases 1 to 5 shipped

The build is disciplined, the security thinking is written down, and the SEO groundwork is better than most agency sites. Three things are hurting it.

The site ships 106 KB of gzipped text per page and roughly 60% of that is source comments. The fixed nav pill covers copy on every long page. And the homepage spends two full screens before it says what Picsel sells.

Everything below was measured against a local build viewed at 1440px in Chrome. Where a number appears, it came from a command.

All five phases are built and verified. The homepage now sends 26 KB of gzipped text instead of 106 KB. The nav no longer covers anything. The sitemap tells the truth. The site publishes from dist/, so nothing reaches the web unless a page asked for it.

Two items are left and both are yours to decide: the 50 MB of irreplaceable mockup sources in the repo root, and whether two projects need mockup variants. Every finding below carries what happened to it.

01

Speed and payload

Biggest single win

Nothing is minified, and the comments are enormous Fixed

What changed: esbuild minifies every published stylesheet and script. Sources keep every word. 106 KB down to 26 KB gzipped on the homepage.

The comments in this codebase are unusually good. They explain decisions rather than restating the code. They should stay in the source. They should not be downloaded by a plumber on a 4G connection.

Critical path

The page waits on two font providers Fixed

What changed: Lexend and Pixelify Sans are Open Font Licence and now served from picsel.co.uk. One render-blocking third party left instead of two. Typekit stays: its licence forbids self-hosting.

Every page opens six same-origin stylesheets plus two third-party ones: Adobe Typekit for the wordmark faces, Google Fonts for Lexend and Pixelify Sans. Both block rendering. The homepage's largest contentful paint is the wordmark, so the measured load time waits on the Typekit stylesheet, the second sheet it imports from p.typekit.net, and then the font file itself.

The preconnects are already there and correctly reasoned. They reduce the cost of the round trip. They do not remove it.

81 KB above the fold

hero.js is the largest file on the site, and it is decoration Fixed

What changed: Minification took it from 81.6 KB to 13.8 KB. It is a module, so it was already deferred. Nothing further needed.

It drives a canvas that sits behind the wordmark. Stripping comments takes it to about 30 KB. Beyond that, it runs above the fold on exactly one page and could load after first paint rather than as a module in the head.

02

SEO

Wrong data, every deploy

The sitemap says every page changed today Fixed

What changed: Every URL now carries the date of the last commit that changed that page, read from git in one pass. Verified stable across repeated builds.

sitemap.xml sets lastmod from the build date. Run the build with no content change at all and every one of the 26 URLs moves:

-    <lastmod>2026-08-19</lastmod>
+    <lastmod>2026-08-20</lastmod>

Google treats a lastmod that always says "today" as noise and stops reading it. Worse, it hides the pages that genuinely did change.

Fix: derive each page's lastmod from the last commit that touched its source, with git log -1 --format=%cs -- <file>, falling back to the build date when git is not available.

Conversion

The homepage buries its own pitch Fixed

What changed: The hero is 56% of the screen (50% on a phone) and the pinned scrub went from a full screen to a sixth of one. The pitch, the price and a Get in touch button are all on the first screen.

Load the site and the first screen is the wordmark, "Design Studio", and the word SCROLL. The second screen is empty dot field. The first sentence a visitor reads sits roughly two viewport heights down.

The title, meta description and h1 are all correct and well written. Nobody reads them on the page. For a site whose job is turning a Google click into a phone call, a full-screen splash before the offer is expensive.

Already right

What is working, and it is unusual

  • robots.txt names 22 AI and search crawlers individually, with a Content-Signal line. Ahead of the field.
  • llms.txt present and current.
  • Organization and WebSite schema on all 28 pages, generated rather than typed.
  • Exactly one h1 per page across all 28. No exceptions.
  • Alt text on all 69 images. Width and height on 63, so nothing jumps as they load.
  • A typo'd address returns the real 404 page with a 404 status, not the homepage with a 200.
  • No em dashes in any rendered copy, title or meta description. Checked with comments and scripts stripped out first.
03

Security

One file to fix

There are no response headers at all Fixed

What changed: A _headers file ships HSTS, nosniff, a referrer policy, a permissions policy, and an enforced CSP covering framing, base-uri, object-src and form-action. The full source policy ships report-only for a week first.

There is no _headers file. Cloudflare serves the site with no CSP, no X-Content-Type-Options, no Referrer-Policy, no Permissions-Policy and no HSTS. The site loads scripts from static.cloudflareinsights.com and stylesheets from two font CDNs, none of them constrained by anything.

This is the one real gap in an otherwise careful setup. Proposed starting point:

/*
  Content-Security-Policy: default-src 'self';
    script-src 'self' https://static.cloudflareinsights.com;
    style-src 'self' https://use.typekit.net https://fonts.googleapis.com;
    font-src 'self' https://use.typekit.net https://p.typekit.net
             https://fonts.gstatic.com;
    img-src 'self' data:;
    connect-src 'self' https://hq.picsel.co.uk https://api.web3forms.com
                https://cloudflareinsights.com;
    form-action https://api.web3forms.com;
    frame-ancestors 'none'; base-uri 'self'
  Strict-Transport-Security: max-age=31536000; includeSubDomains
  X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff
  Referrer-Policy: strict-origin-when-cross-origin
  Permissions-Policy: geolocation=(), microphone=(), camera=()

The homepage carries one inline <script> block, so it needs either a hash in script-src or a move into a file. Sort that out first, then ship the policy as Content-Security-Policy-Report-Only for a week before enforcing it.

Already right

The rest of the posture holds up

  • .assetsignore keeps worker.js, functions/, tools/, site.config.js, projects.js, pricing.js and every markdown file out of the published assets. The reasoning is written into the file, including why the HQ address in card.js must never reach a browser.
  • The contact form carries both a honeypot and a botcheck field.
  • No innerHTML and no inline event handlers anywhere in shipped JavaScript.
  • card.js validates the ?b= batch parameter against /^v[0-9]{1,3}$/ before it becomes a storage key, and filters crawler user agents out of the counts.
  • The Web3Forms access key and the Cloudflare beacon token are public identifiers by design, and both are documented as such where they live.
04

Design and layout

Every page, every scroll

The nav pill covers the copy underneath it Fixed

What changed: The bar hides while you read down the page and comes back the moment you scroll up. Verified at four scroll positions plus keyboard focus while hidden.

This is the worst of the design problems, because it does not depend on the page or the scroll position. The pill is fixed, centred and opaque, and body text passes behind it.

Caught in the act:

  • FAQ: "Do I need to know anything about we[bsites]" and "How much does a website cost?" both clipped mid heading.
  • Reviews: "We're getting three times [as many]" and a review date hidden behind the pill.
  • The intro paragraph: "get you found on Google and in AI search, and [keep]" cut mid sentence.

Three fixes, in order of preference

  1. Hide the pill on scroll down, show it on scroll up. nav.js already tracks scroll direction for the entry animation.
  2. Give the pill a solid backdrop and add matching top padding and scroll-margin-top to every section, so nothing ever sits under it.
  3. Move it out of the centre. The overlap only reads as broken because it lands in the middle of a line of text.
Work ring

The name and the screenshot fall out of step Fixed

What changed: Both the panel crossfade and the letter build are now shares of the same turn duration the wheel uses. Every letter lands by 350ms of a 620ms turn; it used to take 1.3 seconds on a long client name.

The pinned ring animates the project name and subtitle separately from the card. Mid transition the panel showed "Tree surgery · Website, SEO and Google Profile" with no name above it, while A Nevitt Construction's screenshot was still the card on screen. The glitch effect on the name also renders it as scattered fragments for long enough to read as broken rather than as an effect.

Drive the text swap from the same scroll progress value as the card, and shorten the glitch so the name is legible through most of the transition.

Withdrawn

The ring does not clip at the right edge

I reported this from a screenshot and it was wrong. Measuring every element in the ring at 1280, 1440 and 1920, at rest and sampled across four turns, the widest right edge reaches exactly the viewport and never passes it.

What I read as a clipped card was a card behind the front one, blurred and bleeding off the right edge, which is the wheel effect working. The front card sits inside the viewport with a gutter. Nothing to fix.

Done, and clean

Mobile was tested, and it holds up

Driven through headless Chrome at 375, 768, 1024 and 1440 across nine routes, measuring every element for horizontal overflow and every control for tap-target size.

No horizontal overflow anywhere. No undersized tap targets. The one flag was the contact form's honeypot checkbox, which is 13×13 and parked at left: -9999px where no person can reach it. A false positive, and the honeypot is doing its job.

Dead code and the directory

Done in this pass

The work grid's shape system is gone

The last commit replaced the homepage grid with the pinned 3D ring. That left tools/partials/work-card.js cycling five card shapes nothing rendered any more, and four span rules in site.css nothing matched.

  • SHAPES and the variant option removed. The component now has one shape and one caller.
  • .work-card--lead, --phone, --half, --banner, the .work-grid--index modifier and the :not() guard that existed only to keep the two layouts apart. The 64rem six-column block went with them.
  • .display--centred in base.css and --text-display in tokens.css, both unreferenced.

135 lines deleted, 74 added. The build still produces 28 pages, all 7 tests pass, and /work renders identically.

Kept on purpose

Four unused selectors that should stay

.post__figure, .post__list, .post__table and .post__table-wrap in article.css are unused today. They are the blog's half of selectors whose .guide__ twins are live. Delete them and the next blog post with a table silently loses styling the guides have.

06

What shipped

Five phases, ordered by what a visitor feels first. All five are built and verified.

Phase 1Stop the bleeding
  1. Nav overlap. nav.js tracks scroll direction behind an 8px deadband and one rAF; the bar hides going down and returns going up. The :focus-within escape hatch still brings it back for a keyboard.
  2. Sitemap lastmod. One git log pass maps every built page to the commit that last changed it, with uncommitted pages falling back to today. Two consecutive builds now produce a byte-identical sitemap.
  3. _headers. HSTS, nosniff, referrer and permissions policies, plus an enforced CSP for framing, base-uri, object-src and form-action. The source policy ships report-only until you have watched a console for a week.
  4. Mobile pass. 375, 768, 1024, 1440 across nine routes. Clean.
Phase 2Cut the payload
  1. Minification. esbuild, used as a minifier and deliberately not as a bundler: every module keeps its own file and its own imports, so hq-beacon.js is still one instance shared between the page and contact.js.
  2. One core stylesheet. tokens, base and site are still authored as three files and served as core.css. 88.6 KB of source, 19.5 KB shipped, three round trips saved on all 28 pages.
  3. hero.js. 81.6 KB to 13.8 KB, already deferred. No restructuring needed.
  4. Self-hosted fonts. npm run fonts vendors Lexend and Pixelify Sans with their OFL licences. The build itself never touches the network.
Phase 3Earn the first screen
  1. Hero stage cut to 56svh, and 50svh on a phone. The pinned scrub went from a full screen to a sixth of one.
  2. A Get in touch button and the phone number added under the opening statement, above the small print. Until now the first thing on the site anybody could act on was at the very bottom of the page.
  3. The Scroll cue removed. It earned its place when the hero was the whole screen and nothing else said the page continued; now the page shows you. It was also landing on top of the new button.
Phase 4Finish the ring
  1. Panel crossfade and letter build are both shares of --step-ms, the same value the wheel turns on, so they cannot come apart at any scroll speed.
  2. The glitch has a time budget rather than a fixed per-letter step, so a twenty-five letter client name lands in the same time as a nine letter one.
  3. Right-edge clipping: measured, and it does not happen. Finding withdrawn.
Phase 5Housekeeping
  1. npm run check added: builds, tests, then scans for class names nothing uses, tokens nothing reads, and em dashes in rendered copy. Nothing fails the build; it prints and a person decides.
  2. The mockup sources and ben-laptop.jpg: still untouched, still yours to move.
  3. house-of-cornwall and lanora-house mockup variants: still your call.
